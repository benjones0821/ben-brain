Half true. Reviews matter; ad spend doesn't.

**The ad spend part is wrong.** Paid Google Ads can appear *around* an AI Overview — Google says search and shopping ads that win the auction are automatically eligible to show inside AI Overviews — but those are labeled "Sponsored." You can't buy your way into the organic AI answer, and you can't even target ad placement into AI Overviews directly. Spending more won't get you named as a recommended lawyer.

**The reviews part is roughly right, but incomplete.** For "[practice area] lawyer in [city]" queries, AI tools lean on the same local SEO infrastructure that drives Maps rankings: Google Business Profile completeness and activity, citation consistency, and review density in your market. What your agency left out is everything else feeding the same calculation:

- **Legal directories** — Martindale-Hubbell, Avvo, Super Lawyers and similar have decades of domain authority; when they verify your bar admissions and practice areas, some of that credibility transfers.
- **Third-party mentions** — local news, bar associations, community orgs.
- **Structured data** — LegalService and Attorney schema markup, plus identical name/address/phone everywhere.
- **Substantive content** on your own site about your actual jurisdiction and practice areas.

Worth knowing: Ahrefs found only 38% of AI Overview citations come from pages ranking in the top 10, and 86% of most-cited sources weren't shared between AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. So this isn't the same game as ad rank, and winning on one platform doesn't carry to the others.

If your agency's answer to "why aren't I in the AI results" is "spend more on ads," that's worth pushing back on — it's the one lever that provably doesn't move this.

### Claude · 2026-09-02 14:30

> *Claude used web_search: generative engine optimization law firms study data what drives AI citations 2026*

Here's what the data actually shows, and it sharpens the point from before.

**Money doesn't buy citation weight — but it can be wasted.** A study logging real answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews found Super Lawyers cited in 33% of lawyer-recommendation answers and Justia in 20%, with Avvo, Best Lawyers, Best Law Firms and Attorney at Law Magazine in single digits. Meanwhile FindLaw appeared in roughly 1%, Martindale-Hubbell under 1%, and Lawyers.com and Nolo in none — several of these being the directories firms pay the most for. The most-cited directory can't be bought into at all, and the second most-cited delivers its value on the free tier. Google properties, Reddit and YouTube also picked up citations at 12%, 8% and 5%.

So your agency's instinct isn't crazy — spend is correlated with visibility in the *old* channels. It's just pointed at the wrong assets.

**The other findings worth knowing:**

- A separate analysis calls the dominant sources a citation cartel — a tight group of ranking and profile sites appearing in nearly every AI-generated legal answer, while individual firm websites rarely surface on their own.
- AI Overviews now cite about 13.3 sources per response per BrightEdge's February 2026 data, up from 6.82 in 2024 — more slots, more competition per slot. They appear in roughly 23.6% of legal queries.
- Engines disagree sharply. In one test, ChatGPT's entire cited set for Phoenix and Houston criminal defense was Super Lawyers, Expertise.com and Best Law Firms — meaning if those properties don't list you, ChatGPT effectively doesn't either.
- On paid upgrades: they're worth it when they add crawlable content — longer profiles, practice descriptions, case results — and not worth it for badges that render as images with no structured data. Judge every upsell by whether a machine can read it.
- Keep your state bar profile current; engines use bar data to verify licensure claims even when they don't cite it.
- One caution on the peer-review lists: Super Lawyers uses a multi-stage peer-review and independent research process, and Best Lawyers has run since 1983 — you can't purchase those designations, which is precisely why they carry weight.

A concrete ask for your agency: which specific prompts do they monitor, and what's your citation share on each? Not every agency tracks AI visibility the same way, so asking for the exact prompts is the diagnostic question. If they can't answer, they don't have a measurement system — they have a theory.

A deeper investigation could pull the underlying BrightEdge, Ahrefs and 5WPR datasets, map citation patterns specific to your practice area and metro, and audit which of the seven dominant directories currently list you.
