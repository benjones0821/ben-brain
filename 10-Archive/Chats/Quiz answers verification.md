Here are the answers. The written article on Management Consulted's site confirmed Q4 exactly. Careernomics' guides and the video transcripts are behind logins or on YouTube, so I couldn't read them directly. For those questions I've noted how sure I am.

**Q1 – Changes over the next 20 years:** **Increase in machine learning and AI usage in data analytics**
The other three options go against the trends the video describes. Industry sources say AI is producing smaller, leaner consulting teams. Clients now choose among more options, including **new** entrants, so entrants are rising, not falling. And AI and automation are being used to **increase** efficiency.

**Q2 – Soft skills not required for Information Management consulting:** **False**
Careernomics has a guide for Information Management Consulting. Communication with clients is central to every consulting role, so "not required" is almost certainly the false statement.

**Q3 – Consulting industry is rapidly evolving:** **True**

**Q4 – What is management consulting (Management Consulted):**
- ✅ **Provides solutions and strategies to improve the financial and operational health of an organization**
- ✅ **Third party analyzers that are hired by clients**

This is confirmed word for word by Management Consulted's own page: management consultants are professional experts who provide solutions and strategies to improve the financial and operational health of an organization, and clients hire management consultants primarily as objective third-party analyzers of an organization. The other two options are wrong. The same page lists lack of a creative environment as a drawback, and it says because you're usually working with the largest companies in the world, the stakes are usually quite high.

**Q5 – Three practice areas in the video:**
- ✅ **Management Consulting**
- ✅ **Tech Consulting**
- ✅ **Healthcare Consulting**

I'm fairly confident but not certain on this one, because I couldn't pull the video transcript. "Economics Policy" isn't a standard consulting practice area. "Boutique" describes a type of firm, not a practice area, so it's the least likely of the remaining options. If you can skim the video, check this one first.
