---

### Claude · 2026-10-06 14:36

Personal email. You'll lose access to your school address after graduation (or it becomes alumni-forwarding at best), and Levels.fyi is something you'll use most when negotiating offers and switching jobs years from now. Using a personal address keeps your account, saved comps, and any offer submissions with you.

The one reason to use a school email is if a site gives student-specific perks or verification for it. Levels.fyi doesn't gate its core salary data behind a .edu, so there's no real upside there. If something later does require student verification, you can usually add or verify the school address at that point.

Same logic applies to LinkedIn, GitHub, and any other career account: sign up with personal, add school as a secondary if needed.

### Claude · 2026-10-06 14:37

Put a low number in both, something like **$40,000** for each.

That form is built for full-time job seekers, so it doesn't map well to internships. The two things that matter for you:

- **It's a filter.** Anything below your number gets hidden, and as a sophomore you want to see as many openings as possible. $40k annualized is roughly $19 to $20/hour, which sits below most tech internships, so you won't filter out much.
- **Recruiters see it, but it barely matters for interns.** Intern pay is usually a fixed rate per company and not negotiated, so a low number won't cost you money the way it could for a full-time offer.

Use the same figure for total compensation, since most internships don't include equity or bonuses.

You can raise both numbers later when you're looking for full-time roles, and that's when it's worth setting them carefully.
