**OWYN Pro Elite** is your best bet at Publix — it's the one that consistently lands in the 90s on Yuka. 32g plant protein, 0g sugar, no artificial colors, flavors, or sweeteners, and the ingredient list is short: pea and pumpkin seed protein, flax oil, monk fruit, guar gum. Runner-up if you want dairy protein: **Orgain Organic Nutrition Shake**. It's USDA Organic with grass-fed milk and whey protein concentrate, no artificial sweeteners or carrageenan — the organic certification is worth an automatic bump in Yuka's formula. Downside is only 16g protein plus added cane sugar and brown rice syrup, which drags the nutrition portion of the score down.

What to skip on that aisle:
- **Premier Protein** — sucralose and acesulfame K; people scanning it report around 39/100
- **Fairlife Core Power** — also sucralose/ace-K, scores poorly despite great macros
- **Muscle Milk** — same story, plus carrageenan in some versions

One caveat: Yuka scores are tied to specific barcodes, so flavors within the same brand can differ by a few points. Worth scanning the exact can in your hand.

Go with the 32g — for your goals it's the better bottle, and the Yuka gap between them is small.

**Regular OWYN (20g, image 2)**
180 cal, 8g carbs, 3g fiber, 4g sugar (all added), 20g protein. Additives: sunflower lecithin, guar gum, natural flavor, monk fruit. Slightly shorter list, and it uses organic cane sugar plus organic pumpkin seed protein and flax oil.

Where they trade off:
- **Pro Elite wins:** zero sugar vs 4g added, 60% more protein for 20 more calories
- **Regular wins:** ~50-80mg less sodium, no stevia, a few more organic ingredients

Yuka weights nutrition at 60%, additives 30%, organic 10%. Zero added sugar is a real advantage there; the extra sodium is a real ding. Neither carries a full organic certification, so neither gets that bonus. Net: they land close, likely both in the green 75-90 range, with Pro Elite slightly ahead or tied.

One thing worth knowing — OWYN's own marketing for the Pro Elite line claims < cite index="24-1">"No Stevia,"</cite> but the bottle you photographed clearly lists stevia leaf extract. Formulations differ by flavor and production run, so the label in your hand is the one to trust.

### Claude · 2026-08-26 14:09

Correct — nothing in that ingredient list falls into Yuka's "hazardous" or "moderate risk" tiers. The only additives present are:

- **Sunflower lecithin** (E322) – no risk
- **Gellan gum** (E418) – no risk
- **Monk fruit extract** – no risk
- **Stevia leaf extract** (steviol glycosides, E960) – usually rated limited risk (yellow), the one item most likely to show a non-green dot

So worst case you get one yellow. No sucralose, no acesulfame K, no carrageenan, no artificial colors — the things that tank Premier Protein and Core Power.

Two honest caveats. First, "natural flavor" isn't an additive Yuka scores, but it's a legally vague term that can cover dozens of undisclosed compounds — clean by the app's math, opaque in reality. Second, the additive portion is only 30% of the score; < cite index="19-1">the 350mg sodium</cite> is what will actually hold this one back on the nutrition side, not the ingredients. If you're drinking one or two a day, that's a meaningful chunk of a day's sodium.
