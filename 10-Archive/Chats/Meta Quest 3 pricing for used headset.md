Here's how I'd price each. The logic for a friend sale: you're skipping eBay's ~13% cut, shipping, and the risk of a stranger claiming it arrived broken — so a fair friend price is roughly what you'd *net* selling it yourself, not what you'd *list* it for.

**If it's 128GB — ask $200, take $175**

Opening at $200 gives you room to "give them a deal" down to $175 and still be at the honest top of what the model is worth. Anything under $150 and you're doing them a bigger favor than the friendship probably requires.

**If it's 512GB — ask $300, take $275**

This is the one where you'd feel it if you underpriced. Strangers on eBay are paying $350+ before fees, so $300 is already a visible discount and $275 is generous.

**Quick way to confirm which you have:** put the headset on, go to Settings → System → Storage (or Settings → About). It'll show total capacity. There's also a sticker under the facial interface pad with the SKU on it, but the menu is faster.

One note on doing this with a friend: mention the missing box and anything cosmetic up front, even minor scuffs. It costs you nothing at these prices and it heads off the awkward conversation where they feel they overpaid three weeks later. If it's truly like new, say so plainly and let the price speak for itself.

### Claude · 2026-07-31 17:18

You don't need it in hand. A few ways to check remotely:

**Meta Horizon app on your phone** (the old Oculus app). Open it, go to the Devices tab, select the headset — it'll show storage used and total capacity even when the headset is off and elsewhere. This is the easiest route if you're still logged into the account.

**Your purchase record.** This one's almost as reliable and takes 30 seconds: check the order email or your card statement from when you bought it. At the time, the 128GB was $499 and the 512GB was $649. If you paid around $500, it's the 128. Around $650, it's the 512. Watch out for bundles or sales that muddy the number, but the gap is wide enough that it's usually obvious.

**meta.com account settings.** Log in on a browser and look under your devices — registered headsets show up there with model info.

One thing worth flagging: if the headset isn't with you, you also can't verify the "like new" part right now — lens scratches and controller drift are the two things that actually move the price, and neither shows up in an app. I'd quote your friend the number as a *tentative* price and say you'll confirm once you've got it in hand. Saves you from either eating a loss on a flaw you forgot about, or having to walk a price back after you've already shaken on it.
