I attended the Warrington Career Expo on September 16th because I wanted to hear from real companies and figure out what I should be doing now to reach my goals, and hopefully land an internship for this summer. The recruiter I talked to gave me some pretty basic information about the company, but the conversation wasn't very helpful, and he didn't seem very passionate about it. I was a little surprised, because I expected him to be interested in the work I did this summer, and he didn't seem to care much. Then I went to TransUnion, the only other data company at the Expo, and I could instantly tell the energy was completely different. We ended up talking for about 45 minutes. They were genuinely interested in what I'm currently working on and in my past experience. I got to talk with everyone at the table, including someone in sales, a business development representative (BDR), and their supervisor. The only place I fell short was that they were mostly looking for full-time hires, and I'm looking for an internship, which they weren't really offering. Funny enough, I had never heard of TransUnion before the Expo, but it turns out they're in a deal with the company I interned for this summer, which was a cool coincidence.

This event helped my career exploration more than I expected. The TransUnion supervisor really enjoyed my personality and told me I should pursue sales, which no one has ever really told me before. I always thought my strength was operations, but that conversation made me realize I might be good at both. Talking with the sales employee and the BDR also gave me a better idea of what those roles actually look like day to day. BDRs focus on finding and reaching out to new potential clients, while sales works on building those relationships and closing deals. I'd never really considered that side of a data company before, and it made me way more interested in sales as a possible career path. It also made me more confident knowing there are companies out there that like my resume and would want me on their team, even if the timing isn't right yet.

Looking ahead, the biggest step I'm taking this semester is staying connected with the TransUnion employees I talked to. I plan to connect with them on LinkedIn, send a follow-up message thanking them for their time, and keep in touch so I stay on their radar. They told me to come back to their table at every Expo and check whether there's anything new I can apply for, so I'm definitely going to do that. I also want to keep exploring sales roles in the data and tech space so I can figure out whether it's the right path for me, alongside operations. Overall, the Expo showed me that the right conversation can open up opportunities I never would have thought about on my own.
